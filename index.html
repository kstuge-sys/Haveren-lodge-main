Vestvågøy, Lofoten Islands

Built to face the Arctic.

A house shaped by the weather it stands in

HAVEREN sits on a little island, Storøya, surrounded by old farming meadows looking out on the north sea. Inside, the same discipline continues: timber, stone, and glass, arranged so the mountains and the sea are never far from view.

Designed by architect Niels Torp, the house is a quiet study in what an Arctic home can be — sheltering, spare, and built to last through seasons most buildings are never asked to survive.

Architecture — Niels Torp

What the air does to you here

You'll feel this before you feel anything else about the house.

The first breath

Step outside and the air is cold enough to notice — clean, sharp, straight off the sea. It reaches your lungs before your mind catches up, and something in your shoulders lets go.

A quieter head

There's less to think about here, so you do less of it. The noise most days ask you to carry — notifications, small decisions — has nowhere to attach itself.

Something like relief

It isn't excitement. It's closer to relief — the feeling of remembering you don't have to be anywhere else, or perform anything, for the length of your stay.

Ground underfoot

Rock, water, wind — nothing here is asking anything of you. Standing outside and breathing is enough to remind you that you're part of something larger and slower than your week.

Our favorite activities

What we reach for first when the weather cooperates.

Hiking Ryten and Kvalvika

A half-day round trip rewards you with one of Lofoten's most photographed views, and a beach most people only see from above.

Kayaking the fjord

Storøya's sheltered water is calm most mornings — rentals are a short drive away, no experience required.

Fishing off the rocks

Cod and coalfish run close to shore. Bring a rod, or borrow one of ours from the boot room.

Chasing the midnight sun

From late May to mid-July, the light never fully leaves. Stay up for it at least once — the terrace faces west for exactly this.

Watching for the northern lights

On clear, dark-season nights, step outside and look north. No app, no drive — the sky over Storøya does the work.

A slow drive through the villages

Reine, Hamnøy, and Å are each within an hour. Go for the harbors, stay for the waffles.

What to do on a rainy day

Lofoten weather turns quickly — the house is built for that too.

Warm up in the sauna

Wood-fired and steps from the water, so you can go straight from heat to a cold plunge and back.

Read by the window

The window seat off the main room faces the fjord — one of the best places in the house to watch a storm roll through.

Cook something slow

The kitchen is stocked for it. Local cod, cloudberries in season, and a stove big enough for a proper meal.

Board games by the fire

A shelf of games and a wood-burning stove in the living room — built for exactly this kind of afternoon.

The story of the house

HAVEREN began as a question: what does a house owe the place it's built on? On Storøya, that place is unforgiving — wind off the open sea, winters with almost no daylight, summers with almost none of the dark.

The answer took shape as a low, timber-clad house that reads the landscape rather than sitting on top of it. Every room faces the water. Every material was chosen to weather, not resist.

It's rented out to a small number of guests each season, in the hope that they'll feel the same thing we do standing on the terrace at 11pm in July: that this place was worth building carefully.
